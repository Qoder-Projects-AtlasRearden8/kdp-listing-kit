    Which HTML tags does KDP allow?

    KDP's published list for descriptions: <br>, <p>, <b>, <em>, <i>, <u>, <h4>, <h5>, <h6>, <ol>, <ul>, and <li>.

    Attributes such as style or class are not part of the list and are stripped from the preview. The count still reflects your source HTML, just as KDP counts it.

    Official description formatting guide

    A shape that works

    1. Open with the reader's problem or desire. One or two sentences is enough to earn the next line.
    2. Show the path. A short heading and a small list can make the book's promise easy to scan.
    3. Close with what changes. Tell them what they will be able to do, feel, or understand.
    4. Leave out what KDP prohibits. No URLs, phone numbers, review quotes, promotional language, or emojis. The character counter cannot catch these.

    Try a mix

    The problem, the reader, the format, and the adjacent interest. "Plain-English AI for busy parents" gives Amazon more to work with than "AI AI AI".

    Keep it honest

    Skip competitor names, trademarks, subjective claims, and words that do not describe the book. Each slot is precious; a wrong phrase is worse than an empty one.

    04The right shelf

    Three shelves for the right readers

    KDP lets you choose up to three categories per book. Note your best-fit shelves here while you research, then pick the closest matches in KDP. The exact names in KDP's picker may differ.

    A little research now makes the KDP picker much less mysterious later.

    Browse like a reader

    • Start where you think a reader would start, then follow the shelves down to the most specific honest fit.
    • Choose categories the book genuinely belongs in. The right shelf is better than the biggest one.
    • You do not need to use all three unless each one genuinely fits.
    • Write down the path while you browse; category labels and placements can shift over time.

    05Honest metadata

    A clear AI disclosure plan

    KDP asks about AI-generated content in the book itself. This worksheet helps you make the call once, write it down, and walk into the publishing form knowing your answer.

    The short version

    KDP asks authors to disclose AI-generated text, images, and translations. AI-assisted work—editing, brainstorming, polish—does not require disclosure under its current guidelines. Policies can change, so check the definitions before you publish.
